S0 asks what anyone could have known from openly published satellite fire products. Polar satellites: FIRMS (VIIRS on Suomi NPP, NOAA-20, NOAA-21; MODIS on Terra, Aqua) and Sentinel-3A/B SL_2_FRP; the column gives the earlier of the two. Sentinel-3 is never clearly first: for Piodao it detects 2 min before FIRMS (6.52 h against 6.55 h), for the others 0.2 to 20 h later. Its archive holds only the reprocessed NTC products, not the near-real-time ones. Geostationary: SEVIRI (LSA SAF) for Europe and Morocco, GOES-18 and GOES-19 for North America. Detection time only. Publication comes later: measured from the files, GOES products are created 3 min after the scan and LSA SAF products 17.5 min after the slot start; polar data first wait for a ground station (median 25 min, up to about 2 h) and then for processing: in total 2.5 h for FIRMS (Wooster et al. 2021) and 3 h for Sentinel-3 (ESA requirement). Conclusion: after the detection, the ground and processing chain sets the delay, which supports onboard or in-orbit processing as well as the central nodes. The perimeter rule is the same footprint for every source; it reproduces all seven FIRMS first detections and the Larouco SEVIRI time of the pipeline. To resolve before the T0 is fixed: three SEVIRI pixels at the Larouco reference point at 05:26 and 05:56 UTC on 13 August, with no polar pass between 04:31 and 10:41 UTC to confirm them; two high-confidence SEVIRI pixels in the Oimbra perimeter on 11 August.
