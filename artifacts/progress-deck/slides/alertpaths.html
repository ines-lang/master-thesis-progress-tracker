Public data on the real event: FIRMS (T0 of A) · LSA SAF / GOES (T0 of B) · official ignition and area · SLSTR FRP (to add). No public record for path C.

S0 and S1 are the centralized cases: the ground decides. Path B: a geostationary imager (SEVIRI for Europe and Morocco, GOES for Red Canyon) sees the fire every 10 to 15 minutes; it is not modeled, and its alert reaches the ground segment, with timing from the public products. Path A: a public sensing satellite (EO layer, and from S3 the extended public layer) sees the fire. In S1 the alert goes down with its data to the ground segment. From S2 on it goes by an assumed RF crosslink straight to a central node, only when one is in view; the central node decides and distributes the next tasks, and the alert reaches the ground through any coordination satellite with a station in view. The sensor downlink keeps carrying raw data only. Path C, from S4: a commercial satellite such as OroraTech FOREST follows the same rule; the medium of that link is still open. All paths end at T0, the moment the alert is in the network. Commercial detection data are not public, so path C has no public-record reference.
