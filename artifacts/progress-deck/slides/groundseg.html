Why it matters

S1 is slower than Month 2 assumed, so the S2 central nodes have more to gain.

Month 2: every satellite could use all five polar stations. Six events, every S1 pass to +24 h, 10° mask; processing latency not modelled.

Wait = time from the observation of the fire to the first ground station that can receive the stored data. Month 2 let every satellite use all five polar stations (median 8.5 min, maximum 37 min, no pass over one hour). In reality each satellite downlinks to its own stations: median 25 min, maximum 114 min, 26 of 101 passes wait more than one hour. Most data wait for Svalbard (68 of 101 passes): Suomi NPP, Sentinel-3B and Aqua often wait almost a full orbit. Terra sends its data through the TDRSS relay satellites on a schedule, so a fixed mean wait of 25 min (a quarter orbit) is used. Troll is only a backup for NOAA-20/21 and is not modelled. The medians per satellite pool all six events. US direct-readout stations (Madison, Hampton, Mayaguez) receive a pass only while they see the satellite at the observation time: 10 of 17 Red Canyon passes at 0 min. The baseline uses the real segments plus the US direct readout, one fixed set for all scenarios, applied in post-processing. Values are a few minutes optimistic: first contact is the rise above the elevation mask, and every visible pass is assumed to be used for a downlink. Processing latency (under 3 h for FIRMS and SL_2_FRP) is not modelled. Sources: NOAA OSPO notices, ESA Sentinel-3 data flow, NASA Aqua interface document, LANCE 2010, NASA Earthdata FIRMS URT.
