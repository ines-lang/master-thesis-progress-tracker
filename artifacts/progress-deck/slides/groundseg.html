Wait = time from the observation of the fire to the first ground station that can receive the satellite's data. What changed from Month 2 to Month 3. Month 2 let every satellite downlink to any of five polar stations (Svalbard, Fairbanks, McMurdo, Troll, Kiruna), which no satellite really does. Month 3 uses each satellite's own stations: Suomi NPP and Sentinel-3A/B only Svalbard; NOAA-20 and NOAA-21 Svalbard and McMurdo (Troll is only their backup and is not modelled); Aqua Svalbard and Poker Flat. Terra sends its stored data through the TDRSS relay satellites on a schedule, so a fixed mean wait of 25 min (a quarter orbit) is used. New: the US direct-readout stations of the FIRMS ultra-real-time product (Madison, Hampton, Mayaguez) receive a pass while they see the satellite at the observation time: 10 of 17 Red Canyon passes at 0 min; no such path for Europe or Morocco. The set is frozen for all scenarios S1 to S4 (slide 9). Why three times slower: 68 of 101 passes wait for Svalbard; Suomi NPP, Sentinel-3B and Aqua often wait almost a full orbit. Median wait per satellite: Suomi NPP 47 min, Sentinel-3A 11, Sentinel-3B 83, Aqua 79, NOAA-20 32, NOAA-21 12, Terra 25 (fixed). Values are a few minutes optimistic: first contact is the rise above the elevation mask, and every visible pass is assumed to be used for a downlink. Sources: NOAA OSPO notices, ESA Sentinel-3 data flow, NASA Aqua interface document, LANCE 2010, NASA Earthdata FIRMS URT.
