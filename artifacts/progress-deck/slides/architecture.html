Read from the ground up. Link rule for every sensing satellite (EO layer, extended public layer, commercial layer): it either downlinks its raw data to its own ground station (cyan) or sends its alert by an assumed RF crosslink straight to a central node in view (magenta, brown). It never links to a relay or to another sensor. In S1 there is no coordination layer, so the downlink also carries the alert and the ground decides. Coordination layer (teal, S2 to S4): the 600-satellite, 20-plane Walker constellation. Ringed satellites are central nodes. Each relay links (OISL or RF, amber) only to its central node, never to another relay, so every central node is the centre of a star; central nodes are linked to each other by OISL or RF (thick amber). Central nodes receive alerts, hold the fire and fleet state, decide which satellite observes next, and send the task to the central node that sees that satellite, which passes it on (green). Every coordination satellite, relay or central node, can downlink to the ground (dark red), so an alert or decision can leave orbit through whichever coordination satellite has a station in view; decisions stay with the central nodes. Relays have no sensor link. Highlighted: an alert from the EO satellite over the fire straight to a central node and down its gateway (yellow), and a task from one central node directly to the other and on to an EO satellite (green). The two EO satellites on the left and right have no central node in view, so they only downlink raw data. Ground segment: the real stations of each satellite plus the US direct-readout stations, applied in post-processing. GEO satellites are census only, not modeled. Altitudes are not to scale.
