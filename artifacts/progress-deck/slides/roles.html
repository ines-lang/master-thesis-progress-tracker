Node roles and link rules

Roles decide who takes decisions; links decide who can talk. Every link is simulated as valid, and ground links are filtered in post-processing.

Relay node

Links only to its central node (OISL or RF) and can downlink. No decisions.

Central node

Holds the network state and decides task allocation for its cluster. Swept: count and placement.

Gateway

Any coordination satellite with a station in view; sensors talk only to central nodes.

Roles are set by the architecture, links by physics and terminals. Sensors are all satellites that observe fires: the EO layer (S1), the extended public layer (S3) and the commercial layer (S4); they all follow the same rule. A sensor either downlinks its raw data to its own ground station, or sends its alert by an assumed RF crosslink straight to a central node in view; it never links to a relay or to another sensor, so an alert waits until a central node is in view. In S1 there are no central nodes and the downlink carries the alert: the ground decides. From S2 on, every alert and every task passes through a central node, which decides and distributes the task, to another central node (OISL or RF) when the satellite is in another cluster. Each relay links only to its central node (OISL or RF), never to another relay; central nodes are linked to each other by OISL or RF. Every coordination satellite, relay or central node, can downlink, so alerts and decisions leave orbit through whichever one has a station in view; decisions stay with the central nodes. The medium of the commercial link is still open and marked as an assumption.
